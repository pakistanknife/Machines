New multi-head drill machine prices in Pakistan

All new multi-head drill machines sold in Pakistan are imported, primarily from China and Taiwan. Here is what each tier delivers and what it costs.

Basic gang drill (2–3 head) — PKR 6–10 lakh

Two or three independent drill heads mounted on a single table, each with its own motor and chuck. The operator drills one group of holes at a time, moving quickly between heads. These machines accept different drill diameters per head — useful for step-drilling (pilot + final size) or for workpieces requiring holes of multiple diameters at fixed spacing.

A machine costing USD 3,000–5,500 FOB in China arrives at PKR 6–9 lakh after import duty (~20%) and GST (17%). Suitable for light fabrication and small workshop use.

Mid-range 4–6 spindle — PKR 10–15 lakh

True multi-spindle units where all heads descend simultaneously in a single stroke — drilling 4 to 6 identical holes at once. These suit plates, panels, and structural fixtures where the same hole pattern repeats across hundreds of parts. Output can be 4–6× faster than a single-head drill press for repetitive work. Most common in metal fabrication, electrical panel, and enclosure manufacturing workshops.

CNC multi-spindle (8+ heads) — PKR 15–20 lakh

CNC-controlled units with 8 or more spindles, adjustable pitch between heads, and automatic feed. These handle complex hole patterns, mixed diameters, and depth control automatically. Used in higher-volume production of structural components, automotive parts, and precision fixtures. The capital cost is justified when manual multi-spindle output becomes the production bottleneck.

Import duty note: Pakistan imposes approximately 20% customs duty + 17% GST on imported drilling machines (HS Chapter 84.59). Total landed cost is typically 35–40% above the ex-factory (FOB) price. Factor in inland freight and clearing before comparing to local used prices.

Applications: what a multi-head drill machine is used for

Plates and panels

Electrical panels, control boxes, steel plates, and aluminium panels require multiple holes at fixed spacing — for switches, conduit entry, mounting bolts, and standoffs. A 5-spindle machine drills all five holes in a single stroke instead of five separate operations. On a batch of 100 panels, a 5-spindle machine saves approximately 70–80% of drilling time versus a single-head drill press.

Fixtures and jigs

Assembly fixtures, welding jigs, and tooling plates need precise, repeatable hole patterns. Multi-spindle drilling with a guide bush plate ensures all holes are drilled at the correct pitch with minimal operator variation — critical when the fixture must mate with a known bolt pattern or standard component.

Structural components

Angles, channels, and flat bar for fabricated structures often carry repeating hole patterns for bolted connections. A multi-head machine eliminates the repeated re-positioning required on a single-head drill, reducing cycle time and operator fatigue on high-volume fabrication runs.

Sports goods hardware

Hockey stick fittings, cricket bat hardware, and sports equipment brackets produced in Sialkot often require multiple holes at standard spacing. Multi-spindle drilling is the efficient production method for these small-batch, repeating-pattern components.

Multi-spindle vs gang drill — which is right for you?

Multi-spindle

All holes drilled simultaneously in one stroke. Best when the same hole pattern repeats on every part. Fastest output. Hole pitch is fixed — any pitch change requires a new spindle drive plate or adapter.

Gang drill

Multiple independent heads, each operated in sequence. More flexible — different diameters and pitches per head. Slightly slower than true multi-spindle but handles varied work without reconfiguration.

What to check when buying a used multi-head drill machine

1. Spindle runout

Mount a test bar in each chuck and measure runout with a dial gauge. Acceptable runout for general drilling is under 0.05 mm; above 0.1 mm causes oversized holes and rapid drill wear. Runout is a strong indicator of spindle bearing condition.

2. Quill play and depth stop

Check that the quill moves smoothly with no lateral play. A loose quill causes inconsistent depth and angled holes. Test the depth stop — it should lock firmly and release cleanly. Quill reconditioning costs PKR 8,000–25,000 per spindle.

3. Chuck condition

Open and close each chuck to full range — the jaws should close concentrically and grip without slipping. A worn chuck is cheap to replace (PKR 2,500–8,000 per chuck) but reveals how the machine has been used overall.

4. Motor and belts

Listen for bearing noise and check belt tension. Worn V-belts cost PKR 500–1,500 per belt and are an easy fix. A motor with burnt insulation smell or excessive heat indicates overloading and is more costly to address.

What is the difference between a multi-spindle drill and a gang drill machine? +
A multi-spindle drill machine has multiple spindles driven by a single head that descend together — all holes are drilled simultaneously in one stroke. A gang drill machine has multiple independent drill heads on a single table, each operated separately — holes are drilled one group at a time but the operator moves quickly between heads. Multi-spindle is faster for identical hole patterns; gang drill is more flexible for different hole diameters and pitches.
