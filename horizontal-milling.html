About this Horizontal Milling Machine

A horizontal milling machine has its spindle parallel to the work table — opposite to a vertical mill. This configuration is optimal for heavy stock removal, slot milling, gang milling (multiple cutters mounted on one arbor), straddle milling, and gear cutting with form cutters. While vertical mills have largely taken over general work, the horizontal mill remains the right choice for production milling of slots, keyways, ferrule grooves, and gear teeth.

For a Sialkot tool shop or production house, a horizontal mill earns its keep on jobs that would be slow or marginal on a vertical: long deep slots, multiple parallel surfaces in one pass, gear-tooth profiling with module cutters, and roughing of heavy castings before finishing on a smaller machine. The robust spindle and rigid column also handle larger cutters and deeper cuts than a typical vertical of similar size.

What is the difference between horizontal and vertical milling?

Spindle orientation. Horizontal = spindle parallel to table, ideal for slot/gang/gear work. Vertical = spindle perpendicular, more versatile for general milling. Many shops have both.

Can it cut gears?

Yes — with the right form cutter (module cutter) and dividing head, this is the traditional way to cut spur gears. Some basic tooling can be supplied on request.
