A machine costing USD 7,000–10,000 FOB in China arrives at PKR 13–18 lakh after import duty (~20%) and GST (17%). Add freight and clearing, and you are at PKR 15–20 lakh on the floor of your workshop.

Japanese premium — PKR 80–150 lakh and above

Import duty note: Pakistan imposes approximately 20% customs duty + 17% GST on imported industrial machinery (HS Chapter 84). Total landed cost is typically 35–40% above the ex-factory (FOB) price. Always ask for the CIF price (cost + insurance + freight) and add clearing charges before comparing to local used prices.

What affects the price of a CNC wire cut machine?

1. Controller brand and type

The controller is the machine's brain. A FANUC or Mitsubishi controller holds its value for 20+ years — spare parts are available, local technicians know them, and software is maintainable. A Chinese OEM controller depreciates faster and is harder to repair when it fails. When buying used, always ask about the controller make before negotiating price.

2. Year of manufacture

Each 5-year step back in age reduces price by roughly 20–30% for comparable condition. A 2015 machine with a Mitsubishi controller is worth significantly more than a 2005 machine with similar specs on paper. Check the nameplate — manufacturers stamp the build year directly on the machine.

3. Work area dimensions

The DK7725 class (250×320 mm) handles most small-to-medium workshop work. A DK7732 (320×400 mm) or DK7740 (400×500 mm) commands 20–40% more. Larger tables cost more to transport, house, and service — only buy the size you actually need.

4. Wire diameter capability

Standard machines cut at 0.18–0.25 mm brass wire, achieving ±0.01–0.02 mm. Machines that support fine wire down to 0.10–0.15 mm achieve ±0.003–0.005 mm and cost more to run (finer wire is more expensive per kg and breaks more often). Match the capability to your actual production requirement.

5. Taper cutting angle

Standard taper is ±3°. Machines rated at ±15° or ±30° are used for progressive dies and complex 3D profiles. These carry a 20–50% premium over standard-taper machines and are specialist equipment — most workshops in Pakistan have no need for them.

6. Local service history

A machine that has been maintained locally — with documented service, Pakistani technicians who know it, and locally available spare parts — is worth paying more for. Grey-market imports with no paper trail are riskier. Always ask the seller which Lahore or Karachi dealer services the model.
