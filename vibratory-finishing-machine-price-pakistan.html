What to check when buying a used vibratory finishing machine

1. Bowl liner condition

The polyurethane or rubber bowl liner protects the machine body and prevents part damage. A worn or cracked liner exposes metal and will damage parts and accelerate media wear. Replacement liners for a 100–200 litre machine cost PKR 25,000–60,000. Always inspect the liner before buying.

2. Motor and eccentric weight

The vibratory motor generates the amplitude. Listen for bearing noise — a worn bearing costs PKR 8,000–20,000 to replace but indicates heavier use than the seller may admit. Check that amplitude is consistent across the bowl by placing a hand at different points on the rim.

3. Separation screen

The screen separates finished parts from media. Torn or warped screens cause parts to mix into the media hopper. Replacement screens are typically PKR 5,000–15,000 but indicate how the machine has been run.

4. Control panel

At minimum, a timer should function correctly. Higher-end machines have amplitude control — verify both work before agreeing a price.

Pro tip: Ask the seller to run a batch of scrap parts with media during the inspection. A healthy machine will produce visibly uniform finishing within 20–30 minutes on a standard wet cycle. Any unusual vibration, noise, or hot motor housing is a sign of bearing or balance issues.

What is the difference between wet and dry vibratory finishing? +
Wet finishing uses water with compound or soap to flush swarf and achieve a smoother, brighter surface — preferred for surgical instruments and cutlery where surface quality matters (Ra 0.2–0.4 µm achievable). Dry finishing uses dry media (ceramic chips, corn cob, walnut shell) without liquid — faster cycle time, suitable for deburring and light polishing of sports goods hardware and industrial components. Most machines in Sialkot run wet cycles for the surgical and cutlery sectors.
